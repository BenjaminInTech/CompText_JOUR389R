Towards protection of intellectual property rights
New Straits Times (Malaysia)
August 10, 1999

Copyright 1999 New Straits Times Press (Malaysia) Berhad
Section: Business; Pg. 22
Length: 419 words
Body

THE Government is committed to addressing intellectual property rights
issues and has stepped up its efforts to protect such rights, said
Minister of Energy, Communication and Multimedia, Datuk Leo Moggie.
"We have reviewed Intellectual Property Rights laws in other countries
and consulted other relevant ministries and government departments in
Malaysia on how best to protect intellectual property.
"We are confident that with the necessary protection and enforcement,
our local IT industry will thrive and Malaysia will evolve into a nation
of creators of intellectual property as opposed to being mere observers
and consumers of its growth."
He said infringement of intellectual property has a direct negative
effect on a nation's society and economy as a whole.
Moggie said to ensure the country's IT professionals and knowledge
workers achieve the Malaysian vision of a digital economy, their
intellectual property had to be protected.
Moggie was speaking at a "Donate-A-Computer" function organised by the
Kuala Lumpur Lion's Club in Kuala Lumpur yesterday.
Representatives from 17 schools and 10 organisations received a total of
43 computers jointly donated by Lion's Club and Microsoft Foundation
Campaign, the corporate philanthrophy arm of Microsoft (M) Sdn Bhd. There
are 17 other previous recipients of the computers.
Under this programme, companies are encouraged to donate computers which
can be recycled. Microsoft upgraded these computers with softwares and
hardwares.
The estimated value of the software and hardware upgraded for the 150
personal computers, which had been donated under the programme to date is
valued at RM256,760.
Microsoft (M) Sdn Bhd managing director Benedict Lee said they have to
date contributed some RM750,000 for such programmes.
Without revealing the total amount, he said, the contributions were part
of the collections from legal suits against illegal software users.
Lee, too, echoed the sentiments of Moggie that the use of original
software would encourage the creation of Malaysia's own software.
Moggie, earlier said, it was encouraging to see the continuous efforts
taken by the private sector and the industry in helping to propel the
country towards the digital economy.
"Today's event is about ensuring that all sectors of our larger
community will be well acounted for, in terms of bridging the information
technology gap between those who have the means and those who don't, but
deserve no less."
